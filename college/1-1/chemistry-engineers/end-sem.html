Part B · Q2 · 10 marksWhat is meant by desalination? How is reverse osmosis helpful in treating water, explain with a neat diagram. Compare reverse osmosis with the ion exchange method.+

Desalination is the removal of dissolved salts from saline or brackish water.

Principle: normally, water moves through a semipermeable membrane from the dilute side to the concentrated side. Apply pressure greater than the osmotic-pressure difference on the saline side: water is forced in the reverse direction.

Working

  • Pretreat the feed to remove suspended matter and reduce membrane fouling.
  • Pump saline water at high pressure against a semipermeable membrane, such as polyamide or cellulose acetate.
  • Water passes through as low-salt permeate; most dissolved salts remain in the concentrate.
  • Collect the product water and remove the concentrated brine.

Advantages: compact operation, no boiling or phase change, and removal of most dissolved salts and many other impurities. Limitations: pressure/power requirement, membrane fouling and brine disposal.

(a)

The Standard Hydrogen Electrode is a primary reference electrode whose standard potential is assigned 0.000 V.

The active electrode is platinised platinum, not mercury; the “Hg/Mercury” label in the supplied drawing is incorrect.

Construction

  • A platinum foil coated with platinum black is immersed in an acidic solution with hydrogen-ion activity 1.
  • Pure H₂ gas at standard pressure (1 bar) bubbles over the platinum surface, usually at 298 K.
  • Platinum provides an inert conducting surface for the hydrogen electrode reaction; a salt bridge connects it to the other half-cell.

Electrode representation

\[\mathrm{Pt\mid H_2(g,1\ bar)\mid H^+(a=1)}\]

Reversible electrode reaction

\[\mathrm{2H^++2e^-\rightleftharpoons H_2}\]

When coupled to another electrode, SHE can act as anode or cathode. It supplies a fixed reference for measuring the other electrode’s potential: hydrogen is oxidised when SHE is the anode and H⁺ is reduced when it is the cathode.

(b)

Iron is more active than copper. In moist contact, the iron screw becomes the anode and the copper part becomes the cathode, forming a galvanic cell.

Anodic dissolution of iron

\[\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-}\]

Cathodic oxygen reduction in aerated neutral water

\[\mathrm{O_2+2H_2O+4e^-\rightarrow4OH^-}\]

Rust formation

\[\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Fe^{2+}+2OH^-}&\mathrm{\rightarrow Fe(OH)_2}\\\mathrm{4Fe(OH)_2+O_2+2H_2O}&\mathrm{\rightarrow4Fe(OH)_3}\end{aligned}\]

Further ageing/dehydration gives hydrated ferric oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust). A small iron screw coupled to a large copper surface has high anodic current density, so the screw corrodes rapidly. Use compatible metals or electrically insulate the joint.

Part B · Q4 · 10 marksHow cathodic protection alters the process of corrosion of a metallic structure, explain about different methods of cathodic protection.+

Cathodic protection prevents corrosion by making the metal structure the cathode of an electrochemical cell.

Sacrificial-anode method

  • Connect the iron/steel structure to a more active metal such as magnesium, zinc or aluminium in the same electrolyte.
  • The active metal becomes the anode and oxidises, supplying electrons to the structure.
  • The structure becomes cathodic and is protected from dissolution.
  • Inspect and replace the sacrificial anode as it is consumed. No external DC supply is needed.

Example: zinc anode

\[\mathrm{Zn\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+2e^-}\]

Electrons supplied by the active metal maintain the steel at a cathodic potential. In aerated water, reduction of dissolved oxygen can occur on the protected steel. Effective protection requires good electrical contact, a common electrolyte and enough sacrificial metal to supply the protective current.

Applications: underground pipelines, ship hulls, storage tanks and water heaters.

Cathodic protection prevents corrosion by making the metal structure the cathode.

Impressed-current method

  • Connect the structure to the negative terminal of a DC supply.
  • Connect a suitable auxiliary anode to the positive terminal and place both in contact with the electrolyte.
  • The supply delivers electrons to the structure, suppressing metal dissolution.
  • Oxidation occurs at the auxiliary anode. Adjust the protective current to suit the structure.

The structure receives electrons and supports a reduction reaction, such as oxygen reduction in aerated water, instead of losing iron by oxidation. The auxiliary anode supports the balancing oxidation. The method suits large structures, but needs continuous power and monitoring to maintain the required protective current.

Applications: long pipelines, storage tanks and marine structures.

Part B · Q5 · 10 marks
(a) How to construct a proton exchange membrane fuel cell, mention the reactions of such a cell at each electrode, and list out applications [5 marks]
(b) What is Syngas? How Fischer-Tropsch process is useful in synthesis of fuels, explain. [5 marks]+

(a)

A PEM fuel cell converts continuously supplied hydrogen and oxygen into electricity using a proton-exchange membrane.

Construction and working

  • Two porous catalytic gas-diffusion electrodes lie on opposite sides of a proton-conducting polymer membrane, such as Nafion.
  • Hydrogen enters the anode and oxygen/air enters the cathode. Flow channels/current collectors distribute gases and collect current.
  • At the anode, H₂ is oxidised. H⁺ crosses the membrane; electrons pass through the external load.
  • At the cathode, oxygen combines with H⁺ and electrons to form water. Remove water and heat and maintain membrane hydration.

Anode

\[\mathrm{2H_2\rightarrow4H^++4e^-}\]

Cathode

\[\mathrm{O_2+4H^++4e^-\rightarrow2H_2O}\]

Overall

\[\mathrm{2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O}\]

Applications: fuel-cell vehicles, portable power, backup power and suitable stationary power systems. The membrane separates the gases and conducts protons while blocking electron flow.

(b)

Syngas (synthesis gas) is a mixture mainly of carbon monoxide and hydrogen. Fischer–Tropsch synthesis converts purified syngas into liquid hydrocarbons over a cobalt- or iron-based catalyst.

Process

  • Produce syngas, for example by passing steam over red-hot coke: C + H₂O → CO + H₂.
  • Remove sulphur compounds and adjust the H₂:CO ratio.
  • Compress the purified gas and pass it through the catalytic converter at about 200–300°C and 5–25 atm in the course process.
  • Cool the products, separate liquids and fractionate them into fuel fractions; recycle suitable unreacted gas.

Paraffin formation

\[n\mathrm{CO}+(2n+1)\mathrm{H_2}\rightarrow\mathrm{C_{n}H_{2n+2}}+n\mathrm{H_2O}\]

Olefin formation

\[n\mathrm{CO}+2n\mathrm{H_2}\rightarrow\mathrm{C_{n}H_{2n}}+n\mathrm{H_2O}\]

The course catalyst contains Co with thoria/magnesia promoters on kieselguhr support. The process makes liquid fuels from feedstocks such as coal, natural gas or biomass instead of relying only on crude petroleum.

Part B · Q9 · 10 marks
(a) Demonstrate the interpretative applications of UV–visible spectroscopy in detecting and analysing pollutants of the dye industry. [5 marks]
(b) Describe the role of IR spectroscopy in night vision—security and pollution under control—CO sensor. [5 marks]+

(a)

Principle: methylene blue absorbs visible light. Within the working range, absorbance follows Beer–Lambert law.

Beer–Lambert law

\[A=\varepsilon lc\]

Procedure

  • Prepare methylene-blue standards of known concentrations.
  • Set the instrument at the absorption maximum, approximately 664 nm in dilute aqueous solution.
  • Use a solvent blank and measure the absorbance of each standard.
  • Plot absorbance against concentration to obtain a calibration graph.
  • Measure the effluent sample under the same conditions.
  • Read its concentration from the calibration graph and apply any dilution factor.

Treatment efficiency

\[\%\text{ dye removal}=\dfrac{C_i-C_f}{C_i}\times100\]

In A = εlc, ε is molar absorptivity, l is the optical path length and c is concentration. Use the same cuvette and conditions for standards and sample. Dilute samples outside the calibration range; turbidity and other absorbing substances can interfere.

UV–Visible spectroscopy is therefore useful for measuring dye concentration and treatment efficiency.

(b)

Infrared spectroscopy detects molecular vibrations that absorb characteristic IR wavelengths.

Night-vision security

  • Warm objects emit infrared radiation. A passive thermal detector records differences in emitted radiation and converts them into an image.
  • This allows people or intruders to be detected in darkness. Active near-IR systems instead illuminate the scene and detect reflected IR.

CO pollution monitoring

  • CO absorbs infrared radiation near its characteristic band, about 4.6–4.7 μm.
  • In a non-dispersive IR analyser, an IR source sends radiation through a gas sample cell. A selected-band detector measures the decrease in transmitted intensity.
  • A reference channel and calibration relate absorption to CO concentration, allowing exhaust or workplace-air monitoring.

Part B · Q10 · 10 marks
(a) Explain the working principle and applications of STM [5 marks]
(b) What are self-healing materials? Enumerate their working principle and applications. [5 marks]+

(a)

Principle: STM works on quantum tunnelling of electrons between a sharp conducting tip and a conducting sample.

Working

  • Bring the tip very close to the sample and apply a small bias to produce a tunnelling current.
  • Move the tip across the surface using a piezoelectric scanner.
  • In constant-current mode, feedback adjusts the tip height to maintain constant current.
  • Record the height changes to obtain a surface image.

The main components are a sharp conducting tip, bias supply, piezoelectric scanner, current detector, feedback controller and computer. Tunnelling current changes very strongly with tip–sample separation, making small surface-height differences detectable. Constant-height mode instead records current changes while scanning at a fixed nominal tip height.

Uses: studying surface atoms, defects and nanostructures. The sample must provide a conducting path.

Applications also include atomic-scale study of conducting/semiconducting surfaces, adsorbed molecules, surface defects and manipulation of individual atoms under suitable conditions.

(b)

Self-healing materials possess a built-in ability to repair damage and restore their performance.

Working principle

  • Triggering: damage activates the healing mechanism.
  • Transportation: healing material moves into the damaged region.
  • Chemical repair: polymerisation or bond re-formation closes the crack.

In a microcapsule system, a crack breaks embedded capsules. The healing agent flows into the crack and reacts to form a solid repair. In intrinsic systems, reversible bonds reconnect, often with heating.

Capsule-based and vascular systems are extrinsic: healing material is stored in capsules or channels within the matrix. In intrinsic systems, repair arises from reversible bonds or interactions in the polymer itself. Healing can be autonomic, triggered by damage, or activated externally by heat or another stimulus.

Examples: protective coatings, polymer composites, concrete and scratch-repair coatings.

Thermosetting plastics form a rigid, covalently cross-linked three-dimensional network during curing. Heating cannot release the chains for remoulding; excessive heat causes decomposition rather than reversible melting. Example: Bakelite.

Part A · (c) · 2 marksArrange n-octane, naphthalene, iso-octane in increasing order of their knocking tendency.+

Impurities form tiny galvanic cells with the base metal in the presence of an electrolyte. The more active regions become anodes and dissolve, while the impurity-rich cathodic regions support reduction. Hence local corrosion is usually faster than in the pure metal.

Part B · Q2 · 10 marksWhat is Potable water? Explain the steps taken to obtain pure drinking water?+

Potable water is water safe and suitable for drinking: clear, palatable, free from disease-causing organisms and harmful amounts of chemicals.

Treatment sequence

  • Screening: pass raw water through screens to remove leaves, sticks and other large floating matter.
  • Coagulation and flocculation: add a suitable coagulant such as alum; gelatinous Al(OH)₃ traps fine suspended/colloidal impurities in larger flocs.
  • Sedimentation: allow the flocs and suspended solids to settle; withdraw the clarified water.
  • Filtration: pass it through a sand/gravel bed to remove remaining suspended material and reduce microorganisms.
  • Disinfection: chlorinate or use another suitable process to destroy pathogens; provide a controlled residual for distribution.

A sand filter contains fine sand above coarser sand and supporting gravel, with an under-drain to collect filtered water. Retained particles and the surface layer improve filtration. Slow filters are cleaned by removing the clogged surface sand; rapid filters are cleaned by backwashing. Filtration reduces microbial loading, but disinfection is still needed.

Alum coagulation

\[\mathrm{Al_2(SO_4)_3+3Ca(HCO_3)_2\rightarrow2Al(OH)_3\downarrow+3CaSO_4+6CO_2}\]

Chlorination

\[\mathrm{Cl_2+H_2O\rightleftharpoons HCl+HOCl}\]

HOCl provides the main disinfecting action. Store and distribute the treated water hygienically and check its microbial and chemical quality. Ordinary sand filtration does not remove all dissolved salts; add specific treatment where raw-water quality requires it.

Wet or electrochemical corrosion occurs when a metal contacts moisture or an electrolyte. Anodic and cathodic regions develop on the surface. Metal dissolves at the anode, electrons travel through the metal, and reduction occurs at the cathode.

Anodic dissolution

\[\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-}\]

Small surface differences, impurities or unequal oxygen concentration establish local electrochemical cells. An electrical path through the metal and an ionic path through the electrolyte complete the circuit. Only the anodic areas lose iron directly.

Hydrogen-evolution mechanism: in an acidic, poorly aerated medium, hydrogen ions accept electrons at the cathode.

Cathodic reaction

\[\mathrm{2H^++2e^-\rightarrow H_2\uparrow}\]

Overall acid-corrosion reaction

\[\mathrm{Fe+2H^+\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+H_2\uparrow}\]

Hydrogen ions are consumed at the cathode and hydrogen bubbles escape, while ferrous ions enter the solution at the anode. The cathodic process is therefore hydrogen evolution rather than rust formation itself.

Cathodic reaction

\[\mathrm{O_2+2H_2O+4e^-\rightarrow4OH^-}\]

\[\mathrm{Fe^{2+}+2OH^-\rightarrow Fe(OH)_2}\]

Further oxidation

\[\mathrm{4Fe(OH)_2+O_2+2H_2O\rightarrow4Fe(OH)_3}\]

The oxygen-poor region usually acts as the anode, while the better-aerated region supports oxygen reduction. Ferrous ions and hydroxide ions meet in the electrolyte to form ferrous hydroxide, which is further oxidised in the presence of oxygen and water.

Ferric hydroxide undergoes dehydration and ageing to form hydrated ferric oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust). Rust is porous and non-protective, allowing corrosion to continue.

Rust is porous and non-protective, so oxygen and moisture continue to reach the metal. Unequal oxygen access, dissolved salts and small anodic regions promote local attack. (a) What are the factors that lead to caustic embrittlement in boilers? How can this have prevented? [4 marks]
(b) What is the desalination? Explain the desalination of brackish water by Reverse osmosis method. [6 marks]+

(a)

Caustic embrittlement is intergranular cracking of stressed boiler steel caused by concentrated caustic alkali, particularly at joints, bends and fine cracks.

Factors

  • Sodium carbonate conditioning can produce alkaline conditions and NaOH.
  • Boiler water enters fine cracks by capillary action. Evaporation concentrates the alkali there.
  • High local NaOH concentration and tensile stress promote attack and cracking.

Carbonate hydrolysis

\[\mathrm{Na_2CO_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons NaHCO_3+NaOH}\]

Prevention

  • Use phosphate instead of carbonate conditioning where appropriate.
  • Control free-caustic alkalinity and boiler-water chemistry.
  • The course inhibitors tannin/lignin or sodium sulphate reduce alkali entry into fine cracks.
  • Reduce stress concentration and maintain proper water treatment and operating conditions.

(b)

Desalination is the removal of dissolved salts from saline or brackish water.

Principle: normally, water moves through a semipermeable membrane from the dilute side to the concentrated side. Apply pressure greater than the osmotic-pressure difference on the saline side: water is forced in the reverse direction.

Working

  • Pretreat the feed to remove suspended matter and reduce membrane fouling.
  • Pump saline water at high pressure against a semipermeable membrane, such as polyamide or cellulose acetate.
  • Water passes through as low-salt permeate; most dissolved salts remain in the concentrate.
  • Collect the product water and remove the concentrated brine.

Advantages: compact operation, no boiling or phase change, and removal of most dissolved salts and many other impurities. Limitations: pressure/power requirement, membrane fouling and brine disposal.

Ultimate analysis determines the percentages of carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, sulphur and oxygen in coal. It supports fuel-quality assessment, combustion-air calculations and pollution control.

Carbon and hydrogen

  • Burn a known mass m of coal completely in oxygen. Carbon forms CO₂ and hydrogen forms H₂O.
  • Pass products through weighed anhydrous CaCl₂ and KOH absorbers: CaCl₂ gains the water mass and KOH gains the CO₂ mass.

Carbon and hydrogen percentages

\[\%C=\dfrac{12\,m_{CO_2}}{44m}\times100,\qquad\%H=\dfrac{2\,m_{H_2O}}{18m}\times100\]

Nitrogen: in the Kjeldahl method, digest coal with concentrated H₂SO₄ and suitable catalyst/salts to convert nitrogen to ammonium sulphate. Add excess NaOH, distil NH₃ into standard acid and back-titrate the unused acid with a blank correction.

Ammonia liberation

\[\mathrm{(NH_4)_2SO_4+2NaOH\rightarrow Na_2SO_4+2NH_3+2H_2O}\]

Nitrogen

\[\%N=\dfrac{1.4VN}{m}\]

Here V is net mL of acid of normality N consumed by NH₃, and m is coal mass in grams.

Sulphur: burn coal in oxygen in a bomb, extract the sulphate and precipitate it with BaCl₂. Filter, wash, dry and weigh BaSO₄.

Sulphate precipitation

\[\mathrm{Ba^{2+}+SO_4^{2-}\rightarrow BaSO_4\downarrow}\]

Lithium-ion batteries are preferred in electric vehicles because they combine high gravimetric/volumetric energy density with rechargeability.

Advantages

(b)

A methanol–oxygen fuel cell converts the chemical energy of methanol and oxygen directly into electrical energy.

Construction: two catalytic electrodes are separated by a proton-exchange membrane such as Nafion. The anode contains platinum–ruthenium catalyst and the cathode contains platinum catalyst.

Working

  • Aqueous methanol is supplied to the anode, where it is oxidised to carbon dioxide, protons and electrons.
  • Protons pass through the membrane to the cathode.
  • Electrons pass through the external circuit and produce current.
  • Oxygen supplied to the cathode combines with protons and electrons to form water.

Anode

\[\mathrm{CH_3OH+H_2O\rightarrow CO_2+6H^++6e^-}\]

Cathode

\[\mathrm{\dfrac32O_2+6H^++6e^-\rightarrow3H_2O}\]

Overall

\[\boxed{\mathrm{CH_3OH+\dfrac32O_2\rightarrow CO_2+2H_2O}}\]

The membrane conducts protons but does not provide the external electron path. Continuous supply of methanol and oxygen maintains operation, while carbon dioxide and water are removed. The fuel cell therefore produces electricity directly without first converting the fuel energy into mechanical work.

Part B · Q7 · 10 marks
(a) What is galvanic corrosion and how it can be prevented. [4 marks]
(b) Explain the electro chemical theory of corrosion of metals with special reference to rusting of iron. [6 marks]+

(a)

Galvanic corrosion occurs when two dissimilar metals are electrically connected in an electrolyte. The more active metal becomes the anode and dissolves; the more noble metal acts as the cathode.

Example: an iron screw in a copper fitting. In aerated neutral water, the reactions are:

Anode

\[\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-}\]

Cathode

\[\mathrm{O_2+2H_2O+4e^-\rightarrow4OH^-}\]

Electrons flow through the metallic connection from iron to copper; ionic movement in the electrolyte completes the circuit. Attack is severe for a small anode coupled to a large cathode.

Prevention

  • Use the same metal or metals close in the galvanic series.
  • Electrically insulate dissimilar-metal joints and keep them dry.
  • Avoid a small anodic area connected to a large cathodic area; use suitable coatings/protection.

(b)

Wet or electrochemical corrosion occurs when a metal contacts moisture or an electrolyte. Anodic and cathodic regions develop on the surface. Metal dissolves at the anode, electrons travel through the metal, and reduction occurs at the cathode.

Anodic dissolution

\[\mathrm{Fe\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+2e^-}\]

Small surface differences, impurities or unequal oxygen concentration establish local electrochemical cells. An electrical path through the metal and an ionic path through the electrolyte complete the circuit. Only the anodic areas lose iron directly.

Hydrogen-evolution mechanism: in an acidic, poorly aerated medium, hydrogen ions accept electrons at the cathode.

Cathodic reaction

\[\mathrm{2H^++2e^-\rightarrow H_2\uparrow}\]

Overall acid-corrosion reaction

\[\mathrm{Fe+2H^+\rightarrow Fe^{2+}+H_2\uparrow}\]

Hydrogen ions are consumed at the cathode and hydrogen bubbles escape, while ferrous ions enter the solution at the anode. The cathodic process is therefore hydrogen evolution rather than rust formation itself.

Oxygen-absorption mechanism: in aerated neutral or alkaline water, dissolved oxygen accepts electrons at cathodic regions.

Cathodic reaction

\[\mathrm{O_2+2H_2O+4e^-\rightarrow4OH^-}\]

Ferrous hydroxide formation

\[\mathrm{Fe^{2+}+2OH^-\rightarrow Fe(OH)_2}\]

Further oxidation

\[\mathrm{4Fe(OH)_2+O_2+2H_2O\rightarrow4Fe(OH)_3}\]

The oxygen-poor region usually acts as the anode, while the better-aerated region supports oxygen reduction. Ferrous ions and hydroxide ions meet in the electrolyte to form ferrous hydroxide, which is further oxidised in the presence of oxygen and water.

Ferric hydroxide undergoes dehydration and ageing to form hydrated ferric oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust). Rust is porous and non-protective, allowing corrosion to continue.

Part B · Q10 · 10 marks
(a) What are self-healing materials? Discuss the principle and applications of self-healing materials. [6 marks]
(b) What are Biodegradable lubricants? How are they superior to conventional lubricants. [4 marks]+

(a)

Self-healing materials possess a built-in ability to repair damage and restore their performance.

Working principle

  • Triggering: damage activates the healing mechanism.
  • Transportation: healing material moves into the damaged region.
  • Chemical repair: polymerisation or bond re-formation closes the crack.

In a microcapsule system, a crack breaks embedded capsules. The healing agent flows into the crack and reacts to form a solid repair. In intrinsic systems, reversible bonds reconnect, often with heating.

Capsule-based and vascular systems are extrinsic: healing material is stored in capsules or channels within the matrix. In intrinsic systems, repair arises from reversible bonds or interactions in the polymer itself. Healing can be autonomic, triggered by damage, or activated externally by heat or another stimulus.

Examples: protective coatings, polymer composites, concrete and scratch-repair coatings.

(b)

Biodegradable lubricants break down by biological action under suitable conditions. Examples include suitably formulated vegetable-oil lubricants and biodegradable synthetic esters.

Advantages over conventional mineral-oil lubricants

  • Lower persistence after accidental release, reducing long-term environmental contamination.
  • Often derived partly from renewable feedstocks, such as vegetable oils.
  • Suitable formulations offer good lubricity and can reduce friction and wear.
  • Useful in agriculture, forestry, marine and other environmentally sensitive applications.

Biodegradability does not automatically mean non-toxic or superior in every property. Oxidation stability, cold-flow behaviour, hydrolytic stability and compatibility must be checked; appropriate additives/formulation may be required.
