Water and its Treatment · 11 September 2025

WATER AND ITS TREATMENT

Water is the nature’s most wonderful, abundant and useful compound. Water is essential for the survival of all the living beings on earth i.e., man, animals and plants. Water is not only essential for the survival of life, but it is also used for the operation in a large number of industries as coolant, solvent, for steam generation, for air conditioning, firefighting etc. Water is also used for all domestic purposes like bathing, drinking, washing, sanitary, irrigation etc.

Sources of water

The two important sources of water are

(1) Surface water

The water available on the earth’s surface is called as surface water.

Surface water includes rainwater, river water, lake water and seawater.

(2) Underground water

Underground water includes water present between the rocks in the

earth crust, spring water, well water etc.

Impurities present in the water

Chemically pure water is composed of 2 parts of Hydrogen and 1 part of Oxygen by volume and dissolves many substances. So natural water is not pure it dissolves many impurities and broadly classified as

1. Suspended matter-

It is of two types Inorganic suspended matter- clay and sand Organic suspended matter- animal and vegetable matter Turbidity of river water is mainly due to this suspended matter.

2. Dissolved substances

They are classified as Inorganic salts like chlorides, sulphates, carbonates of Ca and Mg in the dissolved form causes hardness to water. Ca and Mg bicarbonates dissolve in water when it passes through the rocks of dolomite or limestone etc., in presence of air.

CaCO3 / MgCO3 + H2O + CO2 → Ca(HCO3)2 / Mg(HCO3)2

Dissolved impurities like salts of Na and K causes alkalinity to water and other salts like Fe, Silica, Mn and alumina and cations like Fe+3 and Al+3 etc may also be present. Dissolved gases CO2, N2, O2, H2S are responsible for odour and acidity, O2 also causes corrosion of boilers.

Total dissolved salts may range from few ppm to thousand ppm.

3. Colloidal impurities

Very finely divided state of matter which is not visible to naked eye is called colloidal matter. Which is mainly due to silica, clay, aluminium hydroxide and micro-organisms (protozoa and vegetable matter). These cause odour, colour and some of these are responsible for disease in human beings.

4. Organic impurities

These include algae, fungi, and plant matter etc. are vegetative by origin and microorganisms, bacteria which are animal by their origin.

Types of water

There are two types of water.

(i) Soft water - soft water readily gives lather with soap and

(ii) Hard water - hard water does not give lather with soap.

HARDNESS OF WATER

Hardness of water defined as which prevent the lathering of soap.

This is due to presence of in water of certain salts of Ca+2, Mg+2 and other Heavy metals dissolved in it. Soaps are Sodium or Potassium salts of higher fatty acids like oleic acid or palmitic acid or stearic acids.

Temporary Hardness is mainly caused by Bicarbonates of Calcium, Magnesium and other heavy metals.

Reactions of soap (stearic acid) with hard water

2C17H35COONa+CaCl2 → (C17H35COO)2Ca+2NaCl

2C17H35COONa+MgSO4 → (C17H35COO)2Mg+Na2SO4

Temporary Hardness: (Carbonate / alkaline hardness)

It is due to the presence of calcium bicarbonate [Ca (HCO3)2] and magnesium bicarbonate [Mg (HCO3)2]. Temporary Hardness can be removed by boiling. When bicarbonates are

decomposed a, yielding insoluble carbonates or hydroxides, which are deposited as a crust at the bottom of vessel.

Heating results in:

Ca(HCO3)2 → CaCO3 + H2O + CO2

Mg(HCO3)2 → Mg(OH)2 + 2CO2

Temporary hardness can also be removed by adding hydrated lime to precipitate insoluble carbonate.

Ca(HCO3)2 + Ca(OH)2 → 2CaCO3↓ + 2H2O

Mg(HCO3)2+ Ca(OH)2 → Mg(OH)2↓ + 2CaCO3↓ + 2H2O

Permanent Hardness: (Non-Carbonate hardness)

It is due to the presence of chloride, sulphate and nitrate salts of calcium and Magnesium [CaCl2, CaSO4, MgCl2, MgSO4 and Ca(NO3)2, Mg(NO3)2]. Permanent Hardness cannot be removed by boiling but it can be removed by water softening techniques like Lime-soda process, Zeolite, Ion-exchange resin, reverse osmosis etc. Permanent Hardness also known as Non-Carbonate / Non-Alkaline Hardness

Units of Hardness:

The hardness of water can be expressed by any one of the following two methods. Units for measuring hardness

1. mg/L

It is the number of milligrams of CaCO3 equivalent hardness present in one litre of water

2. ppm

It is the number of parts of CaCO3 equivalent hardness present in million parts of water.

1mg / litre = 1 ppm

[ 1L of water = 1 kg (density of water =1)

= 1000 gr = 1000 x 1000 mg = 106 mg

1mg / L = 1 mg of CaCO3 equivalent in 106 mg of water]

Usually, the hardness of water is expressed in terms of calcium carbonate equivalents. The formula used to convert the mass of hardness producing salt to the mass of CaCO3 equivalent is given below.

3. Degree Clarke (oCl)

The number of grains (1/7000 lb) of CaCO3 equivalent hardness per gallon (10 lb) of water. Or It is the parts of CaCO3 equivalent hardness per 70,000 parts of water.

4. Degree French (oFr)

It is the number of CaCO3 equivalent hardness present per 105 parts of water.

1oFr = 1 part of CaCO3 equivalent hardness in 105 parts of water.

The above four units are correlated as given below:

1 ppm = 1 mg/L = 0.1 oFr = 0.07 oCl

1 mg/L = 1 ppm = 0.1 oFr = 0.07 oCl

1oCl = 1.43 oFr = 14.3 ppm = 14.3 mg/L

1oFr = 10 ppm = 10 mg/L = 0.7 oCl

Disadvantages of a hard water sample

In domestic uses

Drinking: Hard water cannot be used for drinking, as it does not quench thirst, is also problematic since it affects the digestive system and at the same time the possibility of deposition of calcium oxalate crystals in the urinary tracks is increasing.

Cooking: Due to the presence of hardness producing salts in hard water, boiling point gets elevated, and a lot of fuel is wasted and required more time. Pulses etc. do not cook in hard water. Taste of tea, coffee becomes unpleasant.

Bathing and washing: It cannot be used for bathing and washing purposes as it does not give lather with soap until all the Ca/Mg salts get precipitated. Thus, a lot of soap gets wasted also and the scum or precipitate produced will adhere on the body, bathtub and fabric/ cloth giving spots and streaks. Also, the presence of iron salts may cause staining of cloths.

In industrial uses

Sugar industry: The salts responsible for hardness create difficulties in refining and crystallization of sugar and the sugar becomes deliquescent.

Paper Industry: Calcium and magnesium salts also interfere with the smooth and glossy finish of the papers. Iron salts may even affect the colour of the paper.

Textile and dyeing industry: Hard water cannot be used in textiles, lot of soap is wasted as it cannot produce good quality of lather during washing of yarn, fabric etc. Precipitates of calcium and magnesium soaps adhere to the fabrics.

These fabrics when dyed latter on do not produce exact shades of colour. Ca, Mg, Mn and Fe salts may react with costly dyes forming undesirable precipitates and coloured spots on the fabrics, thereby spoiling their beauty.

Laundry: Hard water causes wastage of costly soap and interferes with the coloration due to the staining of iron salts.

Concrete making: If hard water containing chlorides and sulphates are used for concrete making, it affect the hydration of cement and final strength of hardened concrete. Pharmaceutical Industry: Hard water if used for preparing drugs / pharmaceutical products (drugs, injections, ointments etc) may produce certain undesirable products in them also gives unwanted chemical reactions.

Steam generation in boilers: For steam generation in boilers, hard water creates many problems like:

(i) scale and sludge formation (ii) corrosion (iii) priming and foaming and (iv) caustic

embrittlement.

Three methods of estimation of Hardness

• Soap titration method

• O.Hehner’s alkalimetric method

• EDTA method

Soap titration method (Clark’s method)

Principle: The hardness is estimated by adding a soap solution of a definite strength to a known volume of a water sample until a permanent lather is formed on shaking.

O. Hehner’s alkalimetric method

Principle: The basic principle is finding the alkalinity of water before boiling and that left after boiling.

EDTA Method

This is a complexometric method which is pH dependent (9-10) maintained by a combination of NH4OH & NH4Cl. EDTA is in the form of its sodium salt which yields anion, and this forms complex with Ca+2 and Mg+2 ions.

Ca2+(aq) and Mg2+(aq) ions that causes hardness in water can be determined by complexometric titration with a chelating agent, ethylene diamine tetra acetic acid (EDTA), usually in the form of disodium salt.

A known volume of sample of water is buffered to a pH 10 with ammonia- ammonium chloride buffer and titrated against a standard solution of disodium salt of ethylene diamine tetra acetic acid in the presence of Eriochrome black-T as indicator.

Addition of Eriochrome Black-T (EBT) indicator to the hard water at pH range 9–10 forms wine red coloured unstable complex with Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions of the sample water. The metal ion indicator complex is relatively less stable compared to the metal ion-EDTA complex. When EDTA is added as titrant, the unstable wine-red colour complex forms a colourless stable complex with EDTA and liberates the blue coloured EBT indicator, which indicates end point of the reaction.

\[\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}/\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}+\mathrm{EBT}\xrightarrow{\text{pH 9-10}}\underbrace{\mathrm{[Ca-EBT]/[Mg-EBT]}}_{\text{Wine red colour (Unstable )}}\]\[\mathrm{[Ca-EBT]/[Mg-EBT]}+\mathrm{EDTA}\xrightarrow{\text{pH 9-10}}\underbrace{\mathrm{[Ca-EDTA]/[Mg-EDTA]}}_{\substack{\text{Colourless complex}\\\text{(stable )}}}+\underbrace{\mathrm{EBT}}_{\text{Blue}}\]

Permanent hardness can be determined by precipitating the temporary hardness by prolonged boiling for about 30 minutes followed by titration with EDTA solution as above. The difference in the titre values corresponds to the temporary hardness of the water sample.

\[\text{Total hardness (mg/L of CaCO3)}=\dfrac{\text{volume of EDTA}\times\text{Normality of EDTA}\times50\times1000}{\text{Volume of sample taken in ml}}\]

This method for hardness determination is of greater accuracy, convenience, and a rapid procedure.

Potable water/Drinking water/Municipal water

Water used for drinking should be

1. Clear, colourless and odourless

2. Free from colloidal and suspended impurities and

3. Free from microorganisms, bacteria and dissolved gases like H2S.

4. Water should be free from objectionable minerals like Pb, As, Cr , Mn etc.

5. Turbidity should not exceed 10 ppm.

6. pH should be 6.5-8.5 and colour- 5ppm.

Stages involved in purifying water for drinking purpose are

1. SCREENING

The raw water is passed through screens, having large number of holes, when floating matters are retained by them.

2. SEDIMENTATION & COAGULATION

In sedimentation, the water is allowed to stand undisturbed in big tanks about 5m deep, where most of the suspended / insoluble matter settles down at the bottom of the tank due to the force of gravity (2-8 hrs). Suspended solids sink to the bottom under the force of gravity & form sediments. This process removes only 70-75% of suspended matter. If the water contains fine clay particles and colloidal matter, coagulation is necessary.Coagulation is often the first step in water treatment after screening. During coagulation, the requisite amounts of chemicals or coagulants are added to water to form insoluble gelatinous, flocculant precipitate, which descent through the water, absorbs and entangles very fine suspended impurities forming bigger flocs, which settle down easily.

Coagulants like Alum or FeSO4 provide Al+3 or Fe+3 ions neutralizes the negative charge of clay / dirt and other dissolved particles in the water. After losing their charge the tiny clay particles come nearer to one another and combine to form bigger particles which settle down. For proper mixing of coagulants with water, mixers are employed. And this water is then sedimented.

The clear water from the top layer is sent to the next tank called Filtration tank.

1) Alum [K2SO4. Al2(SO4)3. 24H2O]

Mostly used in water treatment plants, usually reacts with water in the presence of alkalinity of water, if natural alkalinity is not present sufficient lime is added to water.

Al2(SO4)3 + 3Ca(HCO3)2 → 2Al(OH)3 ↓ + 3CaSO4 + 6CO2

2) Sodium aluminate (NaAlO2)

Sodium aluminate will be used for treating water having no alkalinity (PH <7). For better results pH range is 5.5-8.0.

NaAlO2 + 2H2O → 2Al(OH)3 ↓ + NaOH

MgSO4 + 2NaOH → Mg(OH)2 ↓ + Na2SO4

Aluminium floc causes sedimentation and NaOH precipitates magnesium slats.

3) Ferrous sulphate or copperas (FeSO4. 7H2O)

It gives better results above pH > 8.5, if alkalinity is not present, sufficient lime is added to water.

FeSO4 + Mg(HCO3)2 → Fe(OH)2 ↓ + MgSO4 + CO2

4Fe(OH)2 ↓ + O2 + 2H2O → 4Fe(OH)3 ↓

Fe(OH)3 is in the form of heavy floc, causes quick sedimentation.

3. FILTRATION

In filtration tank, the suspended impurities and the microorganisms are removed. In all types of filtrations, the filter bed used is constructed as follows. The filter bed consists of a layer of fine sand followed by the layer of coarse sand, which is then followed, by a layer of gravel. There is a drain at the bottom to remove the filtered water. The layer of fine sand acts as the filtering unit and the other two beds support the fine sand layer. Generally, filtration is done due to the gravitational force. The colloidal impurities are retained by the fine sand layer resulting the very slow filtration of water.

Periodically the top layer of fine sand is scraped off, washed dried and introduced into the filter bed for reuse. Rapid sand filters make use of compressed air for fast filtration.

The filtered water is then taken to the sterilization tank.

4. DISINFECTION

Water after passing through the coagulation, sedimentation and filtration operations still contains a small percentage of pathogenic bacteria. The water used, particularly for drinking purposes, must be freed from these disease-producing bacteria, microorganisms, etc.

The process of destroying / killing the disease-producing bacteria, microorganisms, etc., from the water and making it safe for use, is called disinfection / sterilization and chemicals used for this process are called disinfectants. It can be carried out by the following methods

1. Boiling

By boiling water for 10-15 minutes, all the disease-producing bacteria are killed and water becomes safe for use.

2. Chlorination

Chlorination is addition of chlorine. The sterilizing agents used are chlorine, bleaching powder and chloramine etc.

a) By adding bleaching powder

In small water tanks, about 1kg of bleaching powder per 1000 KL of water is mixed and water is allowed to stand undisturbed for several hours. The chemical action produces hypochlorous acid, HOCl (powerful germicide).

CaOCl2 + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + Cl2

H2O + Cl2 → HCl + HOCl (Hypochlorous acid)

Germs / bacteria + HOCl → Germs / bacteria are killed

Death of microorganisms/ bacteria results from the chemical reaction of HOCl with enzymes in the cells of microorganisms. Death is due to inactivation of enzymes, which are essential for metabolic processes.

The disinfecting action of CaOCl2 is due to the chlorine made available by it.

Draw backs:

1. Bleaching powder introduces calcium ions into water and thereby making it harder.

2. CaOCl2 deteriorates, due to its continuous decomposition during storage. So, whenever

it is to be added, it has to be analysed for its effective chlorine content.

3. Only calculated amount should be used, since it gives a bad taste and smell to treated

water.

b) By adding chloramine

When Cl2 and NH3 are mixed in 2:1 ratio by volume, chloramine is formed.

Cl2 + NH3 → ClNH2 + HCl

ClNH2 + H2O → HOCl + NH3

Germs / bacteria + HOCl → Germs / bacteria are killed

The advantage of using chloramines is that it does not evaporate out easily and can be much more lasting than chlorine alone and consequently it is better bactericidal than Cl2. Chloramine is used for disinfection of water, because excess of it, does not produce any irritating odour, and it imparts a good taste to treated water.

c) By adding chlorine gas

Chlorine (either gas or in concentrated solution form) is added to water in the acidic pH range of 6.5 to 7. When chlorine is added to water, it forms HCl and HOCl (powerful germicide). The hypochlorous acid molecule enters into the living cells of bacteria and kills them.

Cl2 + H2O → HCl + HOCl (Hypochlorous acid)

Germs / bacteria + HOCl → Germs / bacteria are killed

Liquid chlorine is most effective, when applied to filtered water at such a point where adequate mixing is done.

Apparatus used is called chlorinator, a tower having number of baffle plates. Water and proper concentrated chlorine are introduced at its top and they get thoroughly mixed. Treated water is taken out from the bottom.

Advantages

It is most ideal disinfectant

1. Effective and economical

2. Requires little place for storage

3. Stable and does not deteriorate on keeping

4. Can be used at high as well as low temperatures

5. Will not introduce salt impurities in the treated water

Disadvantages

1. Excess chlorine if added, gives unpleasant taste and odour,

2. Its excess produces an irritation on mucus membrane, quantity should not exceed

0.1-0.2 ppm

3. More effective below 6.5 pH and less effective at higher pH values.

Break point chlorination (or free residual chlorination)

Breakpoint chlorination is the point at which the chlorine demand has been fully satisfied in terms of chlorine addition to the water. (or) A point where all the impurities present in the water such as reducing compounds, organic compounds & ammonia are oxidised by chlorine & the chlorine added further acts as a disinfectant to kill the pathogens. In other words, it involves in addition of sufficient amount of chlorine to oxidise

a) Organic matter

b) Reducing substances

c) Free ammonia, leaving a zero-chlorine residual.

It is the stage when chlorination of water should be stopped.

After this point, whatever chlorine is added appears as a residual Cl2 and hence this point is referred as break point dose. The amount of Cl2 required to reach up to this point is referred as break point dose. Theoretically no chlorine is required to be added beyond break point but practically it is added to ensure the residual chlorine of 0.2mg/L at contact period of 10 min.

When the dosage of applied chlorine to water rich in organic compound or ammonia is gradually increased, the results obtained can be explained in four stages and depicted graphically.

➢ Upto A Chlorine reacts with reducing compounds hence no residual chlorine.

➢ A to B: Chlorine reacts with organic impurities and nitrogen compounds like

ammonia to form chloro-organics & chloramines(combined residual chlorine).

NH3 + HOCl → NH2Cl + H2O

monochloramine

NH2Cl + HOCl → NHCl2 + H2O

dichloramine

NHCl2 + H2O → NCl3 + H2O

trichloramine

➢ B to C: Partial destruction of chloro-organics & chloramines.

➢ C: Breakpoint chlorination

➢ Beyond C: total residual chlorine( free & combined chlorine)-acts as a disinfectant.

Advantages

1. Oxidizes completely organic compounds, ammonia and other reducing agents

2. Removes color in water

3. Destroys completely all the disease producing bacteria.

4. Removes both odor and taste from the water

5. Prevents the growth of any weeds in water.

De-chlorination

Over chlorination after the breakpoint produces unpleasant taste and odor in water. These objectionable qualities can be removed by filtering the over chlorinated water through a bed of molecular carbon. Or

A small percentage of activated carbon, SO2, sodium sulphite or sodium thiosulphate, etc. may be added directly to the water and after allowing a short reaction period, it is then removed by filtration.

SO2 + Cl2 + 2H2O → H2SO4 + 2HCl

Na2SO3 + Cl2 + H2O → Na2SO4 + 2HCl

BOILER TROUBLES:

Boilers are used in industries and power station to generate steam. In treatment of water complete elimination of all the impurities is not possible. The impurity that gives rise to

certain troubles will be removed to certain extent. In modern pressure boilers and laboratories, water purer than the distilled water is required.

Some of the boiler troubles caused by the use of unsuitable water are

1. Carryover – Priming and Foaming

2. Sludge and scale and formation

3. Boiler corrosion and

4. Caustic embrittlement

Sludges and Scales

In boilers, because of continuous evaporation of water, the concentration of salts increase progressively and after the saturation point is reached, precipitate form on the inner walls of the boiler.

Sludge is a soft, loose and slimy precipitate formed within the boiler. It is formed at comparatively colder portions of the boiler and collects in the area where flow rate is slow or at the bends. Sludge can be easily removed by scrapping with a brush.

These are formed by substances which have greater solubility in hot water than in cold- water.

Eg. MgCO3, MgCl2, CaCl2, MgSO4 etc.

Disadvantages:

a. As the sludge’s are poor conductor of heat they cause loss of heat.

b. The working of the boiler is disturbed because of chocking of pipes by the sludge.

c. Difficulty in the operation of the boiler;

d. If sludge is formed along with the scale and is trapped within the scale formed and so it

is difficult to remove.

Prevention:

a. By using well softened water.

b. Removing the concentrated salty water from time to time so that deposition of sludge is

prevented.

Scales are hard, adhering precipitates formed on the inner walls of the boilers. They stick very firmly on to the inner wall surface and are difficult to remove with chisel and hammer.

Following are the causes

a. Decomposition of calcium bicarbonate:

Ca(HCO3)2 → CaCO3 ↓ + H2O + CO2 ↑

In low pressure boilers, CaCO3 causes scale formation.

In High pressure boilers, CaCO3 becomes soluble.

CaCO3 + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + CO2 ↑

b. Decomposition of calcium sulphate:

The solubility of CaSO4 in water decreases with rise of Temperature. CaSO4 is soluble in col water and in super-heated water CaSO4 is insoluble, consequently gets precipitated as hard scale on the heated portions of the boiler. This is the main cause of scales in high-pressure boilers.

Solubility of CaSO4 Temperature
3200 ppm15o C
55 ppm230o C
27 ppm320o C

c. Hydrolysis of Magnesium salts:

Dissolved Magnesium salts undergo hydrolysis forming Mg(OH)2 precipitate.

MgCl2 + 2H2O → Mg(OH)2 ↓ + 2 HCl ↑

Mg(OH)2 so found by hydrolysis of Magnesium salts is a soft type of scale.

d. Presence of Silica:

Silica present in small quantities deposits as silicates like CaSiO3 and MgSiO3 (sand filter is the main source for this silica) They stick firmly on the inner walls of the boiler and are very difficult to remove.

Disadvantages

(i) Wastage of fuel- Poor heat transfer from boiler to water leading to increase in fuel

consumption (thickness of the scale from 1.25-12 mm, fuel consumption from 50% to 150%.

(ii) Danger of explosion- The hot scale cracks because of uneven expansion and water

comes suddenly in contact with overheated Iron plates. This causes in formation of large amount of steam suddenly. This results high pressure causing boiler to explode / burst.

(iii) Decrease in efficiency- Valves and condensers of the boilers are chocked due to scale

formation and boiler efficiency decreases.

(iv) Lowering of boiler safety- Over heating of boiler is to be done in order to maintain

constant supply of steam, this makes the boiler softer and weaker, causes distortion of boiler tube and makes the boiler unsafe to bear the pressure of the steam especially in high pressure boilers.

Removal of scales can be done by:

(i) Wooden scraper or wire brush, suitable for removing loose scales.

(ii) Frequent blow down operations for loose scales. The operation actually involves the

removal of very hard water from a tap at the bottom of the boiler and replenishing the water with softened water called ‘make up’ water.

(iii) Giving thermal shocks, which involve alternate heating and cooling to make the scales

brittle.

(iv) Chemical treatment with 5-10% HCl for carbonates and EDTA treatment with which

Ca/Mg salts form soluble complexes.

INTERNAL TREATEMENT:

Internal treatment of boiler water is carried out by adding proper chemicals either

i) to precipitate the scale forming impurities in the form of sludges, which can be

removed by blow-down operation and

ii) to convert the scale forming chemicals into compounds, which will stay in

dissolved form in water and thus do not cause any harm Some of the internal treatment methods used for the removed of scale formation in boilers are

1. Calgon conditioning:

Involves in adding calgon to boiler water, it prevents the scale and sludge formation by forming soluble complex compound with CaSO4.

Calgon = Sodium hexameta phosphate = (NaPO3)6

Na2[Na4(PO3)6] ↔ 2Na+ + [Na4P6O18]-2

2CaSO4 + [Na4P6O18]-2 → [Ca2P6O18]-2 + 2Na2SO4

2. Collodial Conditioning:

In low pressure boilers, organic substances such as Kerosene, tannin, agar-agar (a gel) etc., are added to prevent the scale formation. These substances gets coated over the scale forming precipitates and gives a loose and non-sticky precipitates which can be removed by using blow-down operation.

3.Phosphate Conditioning:

In high pressure boilers, the addition of sodium phosphate in hard water reacts with the hardness causing agents and gives calcium and magnesium phosphates which are non- adherent soft sludge can be removed easily by blow-down operation.

3CaCl2 + 2 Na3PO4 → Ca3(PO4)2 + 6 NaCl

The choice of phosphate is based on the alkalinity of boiler feed water

a) NaH2PO4 – sodium dihydrogen phosphate (acidic) is more suitable, if the alkalinity

of the boiler water is too high

b) Na2HPO4 – disodium hydrogen phosphate (weakly alkaline) is more suitable, if the

alkalinity of the boiler water is sufficient

c) Na3PO4 – trisodium phosphate (alkaline) is more suitable, if the alkalinity of the

boiler water is low

External Treatment of water

Ion Exchange or Demineralization or Deionization process

Ion Exchange Resins are insoluble, cross-linked, long chain organic polymers with microporous structure and the functional groups attached to the chains are responsible for the ion-exchaning properties.

In this method the hard water is first passed through an acidic resin having replaceable hydrogen ion (RH+ , functional groups like –COOH, -SO3H etc) to remove the cations [Ca2+, Mg2+] and then it is passed through a basic resin [R1OH-, functional groups like –OH, -NH2 etc.] having replaceable hydroxyl ion to remove the anion. Thus both types of ions are totally removed.

Resins are classified as

Cation Exchange Resins (RH+)

These are mainly styrene divinyl benzene co-polymers, which on sulphonation or carboxylation, become capable to exchange their hydrogen ions with cations in water.

Anion Exchange Resins (R1OH_):

Styrene-divinyl benzene or amine-formaldehyde copolymers contain amino or quaternary ammonium or quaternary phosphonium or tertiary sulphonium groups as an integral part of the resin matrix. These, after treatment with dil. NaOH solution, become capable to exchange their OH- anions with anions in water.

Softening Process:

When the hard water sample is passed through the cation exchange / acid resin, and the removal of cations takesplace in the following way.

2RH + Ca(HCO3)2 → R2Ca + 2H2CO3

2RH + Mg(HCO3)2 → R2Mg + 2H2CO3

2RH + CaCl2 → R2Ca + 2HCl

2RH + MgCl2 → R2Mg + 2HCl

2RH + Ca(NO3)2 → R2Ca + 2HNO3

2RH + Mg(NO3)2 → R2Mg + 2HNO3

2RH + CaSO4 → R2Ca + H2SO4

2RH + Mg SO4 → R2Mg + H2SO4

Calcium and magnesium ions are retained by the cation exchanges as R2Ca and R2Mg, equivalent amount of H+ are released from this column to water. The water coming out is highly acidic because the H+ released by the exchange combine with anion of the dissolved salt to produce corresponding acids.

Then the water is passed through the anion exchange / base resin, where the anions of the acids – bicarbonate, chloride, nitrate and sulphate are removed by the exchanger

releasing OH¯ into water. The H+ and OH¯ released from exchangers get combined and produce water.

R1OH + H2CO3 → R1HCO3+ H2O

R1OH + HCl → R1Cl + H2O

R1OH + HNO3 → R1NO3 + H2O

2R1OH + H2SO4¯2 → R12SO4 + 2 H2O

The quality of water obtained by this method is equivalent to distilled water.

Regeneration of Acidic Resin and Basic Resin:

After a long use, the exhausted acidic resin can be regenerated by washing it with dilute solution of Hydrochloric acid or sulphuric acid.

R2Ca + 2HCl → 2RH +CaCl2

The column is washed with deionised water and washing is passed to sink or drain. The exhausted basic resin after a long use can be regenerated by washing it with a dilute solution of NaOH.

R1Cl + NaOH → R1OH + NaCl

R12SO4 + 2NaOH → 2R1OH + Na2SO4

The column is washed with deionised water and washing is passed to sink or drain. The regenerated ion exchange resins are then used again.

Advantages

1) In this method, both types of hardness are removed.

2) The quality of water obtained is equivalent to distilled water and hardness is very low (2

ppm) and the water can be suitable for use in high pressure boiler.

3) There is no wastage of water.

4) This process can be used to soften highly acidic or alkaline waters.

5) Regeneration of ion resins are possible

Disadvantage:

Cost of equipment is high

Highly turbid water cannot be treated

Expensive chemicals are required.

Desalination of brackish water

The process of removing common salt from the water is known as desalination. The water containing dissolved salts with a peculiar salty taste is called brackish water. Sea water, containing on an average about 3.5% salts, comes under this category. Brackish water is totally unfit for drinking purpose. Commonly used methods for the desalination of brackish water is: 1. Reverse Osmosis & 2. Electrodialysis.

Reverse Osmosis (or) Super Filtration (or) Hyper Filtration

Osmosis is the phenomenon by virtue of which flow of solvent takes place from a region of low concentration to high concentration when two solutions of different concentrations are separated by a semi-permeable membrane.

The flow continues till the concentration is equal on both the sides. The driving force for osmosis is osmotic pressure. However, if a hydrostatic pressure in excess of osmotic pressure is applied on the concentrated side, the flow of solvent reverses as it is forced to move from concentrated side to dilute side across the membrane. This is the basis of reverse osmosis.

For practical purposes, semi-permeable membrane based on thin films of cellulose acetate, polymethacrylate and polyamide polymers are used. A pressure of the order of 15-40 kg/cm2 is applied for separating the water from its contaminants. The process is also known as “super-filtration or hyper-filtration”.

Advantages:

a. Colloidal SiO2 can be removed by reverse osmosis which even cannot be removed by

demineralization.

b. It is simple and reliable process.

c. Capital and operating expenses are low.

d. The life of the semi-permeable membrane is about 2 years and it can be easily replaced

within a few minutes, thereby nearly uninterrupted water supply can be provided. e. This process will remove ionic, non-ionic, colloidal and high molecular weight organic matter.

Membrane water treatment is a popular technology used to generate clean drinking water by removing contaminants and impurities. Membrane separation is a physical separation technique based on pore size, molecule size, and surface hydrophilicity or hydrophobicity. It is all about using barriers, or membranes, to filter out unwanted particles and molecules from water. The process of membrane water treatment is made possible through various categories of pressure-driven membrane processes. These processes, namely microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, and reverse osmosis, aim to separate particles and impurities from water through hydraulic pressure.

Membrane water treatment offers several benefits in comparison to traditional treatment methods. Some advantages include the ability to remove particles as small as viruses, improved efficiency, and reduced chemical usage. The use of membrane water treatment can also lead to increased sustainability as it requires less energy and produces less waste. It also includes the production of clean drinking water and the ability to recycle and reuse water.
