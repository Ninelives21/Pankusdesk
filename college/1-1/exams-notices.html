EXAMINATIONS & EVALUATION SECTION

COMPREHENSIVE GUIDELINES TO STUDENTS

B.TECH. CBCS R25 – EXAMINATIONS, EVALUATION, RESULTS & ACADEMIC

PROGRESSION Academic Year 2026–27

PURPOSE OF THIS HANDBOOK

• To guide students in understanding the complete examination cycle, from registration and preparation through the examination, evaluation and declaration of results.

• To provide a single, comprehensive checklist for all examination-related activities.

• This handbook applies to students governed by the B.Tech. CBCS R25 Regulations.

• The VNRVJIET B.Tech. CBCS R25 Academic Regulations shall be the governing document and shall prevail in all matters. This handbook is intended only as a simplified guide to help students understand examination and evaluation-related processes and shall not be treated as a separate set of Regulations.

1. IMPORTANT INSTRUCTIONS FOR STUDENTS

• Read all Examination Notifications issued by the Examination & Evaluation Section and follow the prescribed dates, fees and procedures.

• Complete examination registration within the notified schedule. Before final submission, carefully verify your registered courses, examination category and personal details.

• Check the Institute Examination Portal, Student Login, Institute Email and official notices regularly for updates regarding hall tickets, examination timetables, results, supplementary examinations and other examination-related matters.

• Maintain the required attendance and complete all academic requirements before appearing for the Semester End Examinations (SEE).

• Pay all applicable tuition fees, examination fees and other Institute dues within the prescribed time. Failure to clear the dues may result in withholding results.

• Keep your Student ID Card and Hall Ticket safely and carry them as required for every examination.

• Report any discrepancy in the Hall Ticket, exam registration, examination details, results or personal information to the Examination & Evaluation Section immediately.

PDF page 3

• Maintain strict discipline during all examinations. Any case of malpractice or use of unfair means will be dealt with as per the applicable Institute Regulations.

• Take responsibility for following all examination-related instructions and deadlines. Failure to comply with the notified procedures may affect your eligibility to appear for examinations or receive examination-related services.

2. EXAMINATION ELIGIBILITY

2.1 Attendance

• A minimum of 75% aggregate attendance in all registered courses during the semester is required to appear for the Regular Semester End Examinations (SEE).

• Attendance shortage between 65% and 75% may be condoned by the Institute Academic Committee on genuine and valid grounds, subject to payment of the prescribed condonation fee.

• Attendance below 65% cannot be condoned.

• If the attendance shortage is not condoned, the student will be detained and the semester registration will be cancelled, including the CIE marks. Such a student will not be permitted to appear for the SEE or be promoted to the next semester.

• A student who is under disciplinary suspension will not be permitted to appear for the SEE.

• Students must complete examination registration within the schedule notified by the Examination Branch.

• Examination registration is mandatory for results processing, seating arrangements and preparation of nominal rolls.

• Students are advised to complete registration and payment well before the last date. Any issues with registration or payment should be reported to the Examination Branch immediately.

4. EXAMINATION FEES AND OUTSTANDING DUES

• Students must pay the prescribed examination fee within the notified schedule through the designated payment facility. (Url:https://automation.vnrvjiet.ac.in/examfee )

• Students must download the Hall Ticket from the Student Login or Examination Portal after it is released. (Url : https://automation.vnrvjiet.ac.in/hallticket )

• Students must carry both the Hall Ticket and Student ID Card to every examination.

6. EXAMINATION TIMETABLE AND REPORTING TIME

• Students must follow the examination timetables and instructions officially notified by the Exam Branch.

• Before each examination, students should carefully check the examination date, session (FN/AN), course, examination venue, room number and seating arrangement.

• Students must reach the examination venue at least 15 minutes before the scheduled commencement of the examination.

• Students should plan their travel in advance, taking into account traffic, weather and other local conditions.

• Students must sit only in the seat allotted to them as per the seating arrangement and strictly follow the instructions given by the Invigilator.

8. MALPRACTICE / UNFAIR MEANS

Procedure for Handling Malpractice Cases on the Day of Examination The following procedure shall be followed when a student is caught indulging in malpractice during an examination:

• Identification of Malpractice If a student is found indulging in any form of malpractice in the examination hall, the Invigilator shall immediately identify the case and inform the Floor In-Charge and Observer, clearly stating the nature of malpractice observed.

• Informing the Mentor and HoD The Floor In-Charge/Observer will bring the student to the Examination Branch and inform the Mentor, HoD and parents about the malpractice case.

• Preliminary Clarification from the Student The Controller of Examinations (CoE) or Assistant Controller of Examinations (ACE) shall interact with the student and obtain the student's clarification regarding the malpractice incident.

• Student Consent Form After providing the clarification, the student shall fill in and submit the prescribed Student Consent Form relating to the malpractice case.

• Parent/Guardian Meeting with the CoE The parent/guardian shall meet the Controller of Examinations (CoE) within 24 hours of the malpractice incident to discuss the case.

• Alternative Mode of Parent Interaction If the parent is unable to meet the CoE physically, the parent should interact virtually with CoE. MALPRACTICE AND DISCIPLINARY ACTION Students found indulging in malpractice or improper conduct during any examination or evaluation shall be subject to disciplinary action as prescribed under the B.Tech. CBCS R25 Regulations. The nature of punishment shall depend on the offence and may include cancellation of performance, expulsion, debarment, or other penalties as specified in the Regulations.

9.5. Calculation of Total CIE For every Theory course, the total CIE is: CIE = Sessional Examination marks + CA marks + ELA marks Therefore:

• Sessional Examinations = 30 marks

• Class Assessment = 5 marks

• Experiential Learning Assessment = 5 marks Total = 40 marks

9.6. Computer-Based Test (CBT) A Computer-Based Test (CBT) may be conducted as an additional internal assessment mechanism for each theory course under specified conditions. A student may be eligible for the CBT if they miss one of the two Sessional Examinations due to genuine unavoidable circumstances with valid approval, or if they have appeared in both Sessionals and wish to improve their internal marks. The CBT will be conducted after Sessional Examination-II for 30 marks in 1 hour with 30 MCQs, covering the entire syllabus, with no negative marking. Students must register separately for each subject by paying fee of ₹750 per subject. The marks obtained in the CBT are treated as equivalent to the marks obtained in one Sessional Examination. There are therefore potentially three Sessional-equivalent scores:

1. SE-I

2. SE-II

3. CBT

For calculating the Sessional component of CIE, the average of best two scores out of these three are considered.

9.7. Overall Process in Simple Terms For a Theory course, the process can be understood as follows: Step 1: Conduct SE-I → 30 marks Step 2: Conduct SE-II → 30 marks Step 3: Calculate the Sessional component using the average of SE-I and SE-II. However, if the student is eligible for CBT:

PDF page 9

SE-I + SE-II + CBT → select the best two scores → average them → Sessional component out of 30. Then: Sessional component (30) + CA (5) + ELA (5) = Total CIE (40) In one formula: CIE = Average of best two among (SE-I, SE-II, CBT) + Average of two CAs + Average of two ELAs Maximum = 30 + 5 + 5 = 40 marks Key points to remember

• Every Theory course has 40 CIE marks.

• SE = 30, CA = 5, ELA = 5.

• There are two Sessional Examinations, each for 30 marks and 2 hours.

• SE-I covers Unit I, Unit II and the first half of Unit III.

• SE-II covers the second half of Unit III, Unit IV and Unit V.

• Each SE has 10 marks Part A + 20 marks Part B.

• CA is conducted twice, and the average contributes 5 marks.

• ELA is conducted twice, and the average contributes 5 marks.

• CBT is 30 MCQs, 30 marks, 1 hour, no negative marking, covering the entire syllabus.

• CBT questions are divided into Remember (10), Understand (10), and Apply (10).

• When CBT is used, the best two scores among SE-I, SE-II and CBT are averaged for the 30-mark Sessional component.

• An absence from an SE is normally recorded as zero marks for internal assessment purposes.

10. THEORY EXAMINATION – STUDENT GUIDE

• The Theory Semester End Examination (SEE) is conducted for 60 marks with a duration of 3 hours.

• Part-A: There are 5 compulsory short-answer questions, carrying 2 marks each, for a total of 10 marks.

• Part-B: There are 10 descriptive questions, with 2 questions from each unit. Each unit will have an internal choice, and students must answer one question from each unit. Part-B carries 50 marks.

• All five units carry equal weightage, and the SEE covers the entire prescribed syllabus.

• Students should manage their time effectively to attempt all required questions within the allotted time.

• Students must clearly write the question numbers corresponding to the answers attempted.

11. PRACTICAL EXAMINATION – STUDENT GUIDE

Practical Courses – Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) Each Practical Course shall carry forty (40) marks for Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE), distributed as follows:

• Day-to-Day Performance: 10 marks

• Record Work: 10 marks

• Internal Practical Examination: 10 marks

• Course Project: 10 marks

• Absence in any CIE component shall result in zero marks for that component. No reassessment shall be permitted.

Practical Semester End Examination (SEE) – Pattern and Instructions

• The marks are distributed as follows:

• Initial Procedure and Write-up: 10 marks

• Conduct of Experiment/Program: 15 marks

• Analysis and Evaluation of Results: 15 marks

• Course Project Report: 10 marks

• Viva-Voce: 10 marks

• The Practical SEE is conducted jointly by one External Examiner and one Internal Examiner.

• Students are expected to perform the experiment/program allotted to them and follow the instructions given by the examiners.

• If a student requests a change of experiment/program, 5 marks will be deducted for the first change and an additional 5 marks for the second change.

• A third change of experiment/program will not be permitted.

13. FIELD-BASED RESEARCH PROJECT

• Students shall register after IV Semester End Examinations and complete the project during the summer vacation as per the Academic Calendar.

• The project shall be carried out in an approved industry, research organization, laboratory, or external agency.

• A project report covering objectives, methodology, observations, analysis, outcomes, and conclusions shall be submitted.

• The project shall be evaluated in V Semester through SEE only for 100 marks, with no CIE.

• SEE shall include report evaluation, project presentation, and viva-voce by an Evaluation Committee comprising the External Examiner, HoD, Project Supervisor, and Senior Faculty Member.

• A minimum of 40 marks out of 100 is required to pass. Failure shall result from non- submission, non-presentation, absence from evaluation, or securing less than 40 marks.

14. DESIGN THINKING

• The Design Thinking course promotes creativity, interdisciplinary problem-solving, and application of engineering knowledge to real-world challenges.

• The course shall culminate in an independent project carrying 100 marks, comprising CIE – 40 marks and SEE – 60 marks.

• CIE shall be conducted by a committee comprising the HoD, Course Coordinator, and Senior Faculty Member, based on two seminars as per the Academic Calendar and prescribed evaluation format.

• SEE shall be conducted by the HoD, Course Coordinator, and an External Industry Expert.

• Students shall submit a project report describing the design idea and its realization through a prototype, simulation model, or equivalent outcome.

15. INTERNSHIP / INDUSTRY ORIENTED MINI-PROJECT

• Each student shall undertake an Internship or Industry Oriented Mini-Project as a mandatory B.Tech. Requirement.

• The work shall be carried out in a reputed industry, research organization, academic institution, or relevant industry after VI Semester End Examinations, primarily during the summer vacation.

• Students shall submit a report covering the problem, methodology, outcomes, and learning.

• Evaluation shall be through SEE only for 100 marks, with no CIE, comprising presentation and viva-voce.

• The evaluation committee shall include the External Examiner, HoD, Supervisors, and Senior Faculty Member and assess technical competence, application of knowledge, report quality, professionalism, and presentation.

16. MAJOR PROJECT

• Every student shall undertake a Major Project in VII and VIII Semesters, carried out in two stages: Phase-I and Phase-II.

• Each phase shall carry 100 marks: CIE – 40 marks and SEE – 60 marks.

• Major Project Phase-I (VII Semester):

• CIE shall comprise two seminars and carry 40 marks, with 20 marks by the Evaluation Committee and 20 marks by the Project Supervisor.

• SEE shall carry 60 marks and be conducted by the HoD, Project Supervisor, and Senior Faculty Member.

• Major Project Phase-II (VIII Semester):

• CIE shall comprise two seminars and carry 40 marks, with 20 marks by the Evaluation Committee and 20 marks by the Project Supervisor.

• SEE shall carry 60 marks and include viva-voce, conducted by the External Examiner, HoD, Project Supervisor, and Senior Faculty Member.

• The Major Project topic shall be different from the Internship/Mini-Project, while Phase-I and Phase-II shall continue the same Major Project topic with progressive development.

18. VALUE ADDED COURSES

• Value Added Courses shall follow the evaluation pattern prescribed for theory courses.

• Sessional and Semester End Examinations shall be scheduled separately and shall not overlap with regular curriculum examinations.

• A proctored Sessional Examination of 45 minutes or 1 hour shall be conducted during regular class hours by the course instructor without disrupting other classes.

• Semester End Examinations shall be scheduled and notified by the Examination Branch as per institutional requirements.

19. SKILL DEVELOPMENT COURSES

• The B.Tech. Programme shall include four Skill Development Courses, offered in III, IV, V and VI Semesters.

• Each course shall carry 1 credit and form a distinct academic requirement.

• Evaluation shall follow the laboratory course pattern, comprising CIE and SEE, conducted by Internal/External Examiners as applicable.

• These courses shall enhance students’ cognitive, psychomotor, practical, analytical, and application-oriented skills.

• Departments shall ensure alignment with Graduate Attributes and Programme Outcomes.

21. RESULTS AND MEMORANDUM OF GRADES

• At the time of result declaration, the Institute Examination Portal will display the marks obtained in each course, along with a clear CIE and SEE break-up.

• After every semester, a Memorandum of Grades will be issued to registered students.

• The Memorandum of Grades will contain the Course Code, Course Title, Registered Credits, Letter Grade and Credits Earned.

• CIE and SEE marks will be displayed in results but will not be reflected in the Memorandum of Grades.

22. GRADING SYSTEM

An absolute grading system with a 10-point scale is followed for all courses.

22.1. Passing Requirements

• A student must secure at least a C grade (GP 5) and satisfy the prescribed minimum component requirements to pass a course.

• A student securing F or Ab must appear for the Supplementary Examination.

• For supplementary attempts, the internal marks obtained in the regular examination are retained.

• A semester is considered successfully completed only when the student secures C grade or above in every course registered in that semester.

23. AWARDING OF CLASS BASED ON CGPA

* Refer 23.1 Conditions for First Class with Distinction

23.1. Conditions for First Class with Distinction A student shall be awarded First Class with Distinction if all the following conditions are satisfied:

a) The student has passed all courses in the first appearance (regular examinations only).

b) The student has not been detained at any stage of the programme due to shortage of attendance or credits.

c) The student has not been subjected to any disciplinary action during the programme.

d) A student securing CGPA ≥ 7.5 but not satisfying above conditions shall be placed in First Class.

23.2 Credit Exemption Policy The Credit Exemption Policy provides for:

• 164 total registered credits as prescribed in the curriculum.

• A minimum of 160 credits is required for B.Tech. degree eligibility.

• Up to 4 credits may be exempted from eligible courses.

• Exemption does not apply to Professional Core, Laboratory, Seminar, Project, Field Based Research Project, or Internship/Industry Oriented Mini-Project courses.

• The best 160 credits will be considered for final CGPA computation.

24 REVALUATION AND RECOUNTING

• Students may apply for revaluation and recounting of Theory Courses by paying the prescribed fee within the notified period.

• Revaluation shall be conducted by a qualified evaluator arranged by the Controller of Examinations, and the result shall be declared within the stipulated time.

• Revaluation and Recounting is applicable only to Theory Courses and is not permitted for Laboratory/Practical, Project, Internship, Mini-Project, or other non-theory courses.

25.SUPPLEMENTARY EXAMINATIONS

• Supplementary Examinations are conducted along with the regular Semester End Examinations (SEE) for students who have eligible backlog courses.

• During the SEE of an even semester, Supplementary Examinations are conducted for odd- semester courses.

• During the SEE of an odd semester, Supplementary Examinations are conducted for even- semester courses.

• For courses with F/Ab grades, the regular CIE marks will be retained for the supplementary examination attempt, as per the applicable regulations.

• Students must carefully check the official Supplementary Examination Notification for details regarding eligible courses, registration dates, examination fees, timetable and other applicable conditions.

27. GRACE MARKS

• Grace marks may be awarded to a student who has completed the entire B.Tech.

Coursework but has failed in not more than three theory courses, provided that the award of grace marks enables the student to pass and qualify for the Degree.

• The total grace marks shall not exceed 0.15% of the total maximum marks of all eight semesters excluding marks allocated for Value Added Courses and Skill Development Courses.

• The award of grace marks will be subject to the guidelines issued by the Institute from time to time.

• Grace marks are not automatic or guaranteed. The Examination Branch will determine the student’s eligibility and award of grace marks based on the applicable rules and regulations.

28. CGPA TO EQUIVALENT PERCENTAGE

FORMULA: % of Marks = (Final CGPA – 0.5) × 10. This conversion is to be used where an official/statutory purpose explicitly requires percentage conversion.

29. HONOURS AND MINORS The R25 Regulations provide for B.Tech. Honours and Minor Degree Programmes under the approved curriculum.

• The Minor Programme may be started from the IV Semester and pursued up to the VII Semester.

• The Honours Programme may be started from the V Semester and pursued up to the VIII Semester.

• A student cannot pursue Honours and Minor simultaneously.

• Credits earned through the Honours or Minor Programme will not be counted towards the minimum 160 credits required for the regular B.Tech. Degree.

• Under the Multiple Entry Multiple Exit (MEME) scheme, an eligible student may exit after the IV Semester with a Two-Year Undergraduate Diploma, subject to the prescribed conditions and additional requirements.

• A student who exits under the MEME scheme may re-enter the B.Tech. Programme in the V Semester, subject to the conditions prescribed under the applicable regulations.

1. GENERAL EXAMINATION INFORMATION

1. What is the purpose of these FAQs? They provide quick information about examination registration, eligibility, Hall Tickets, SEE, evaluation, results, supplementary examinations, promotion and degree completion.

2. Which rules should students follow? Follow the approved B.Tech. CBCS R25 Academic Regulations, Academic Calendar and latest official notifications. If there is a difference, the approved Regulations/official notification shall prevail.

3. Which students are covered under the R25 Regulations? B.Tech. Regular/Full-Time students admitted to I Year under the applicable R25 regulations.

4. What is the programme duration?

Four academic years/eight semesters. Maximum completion period: eight academic years from commencement of the first academic year.

2. ATTENDANCE & ELIGIBILITY

5. What attendance is required for SEE? Minimum 75% aggregate attendance in all registered courses of the semester.

6. Can shortage be condoned?

Shortage between 65% and 75% may be condoned by the Institute Academic Committee on genuine and valid grounds, subject to prescribed condonation fee.

7. Can attendance below 65% be condoned? No.

8. What happens if shortage is not condoned?

The student is detained; semester registration is cancelled, including CIE marks, and the student cannot appear for SEE or be promoted.

9. Can a student under disciplinary suspension write SEE? No.

10. What are the minimum academic requirements? SEE component: 21/60; CIE + SEE: 40/100; minimum course grade: C.

3. EXAMINATION REGISTRATION

11. Why is registration important? It is essential for examination processing, seating arrangements, nominal rolls and processing of marks.

12. What should I verify? Course code, course title, semester, examination category, registered courses and payment/registration details.

13. What should I do if I find an error in my examination-related details or documents? Students should report the error immediately to the Examination & Evaluation Section through the prescribed procedure and official channels.

14. Should I wait until the last date? No. Complete registration and payment well before the deadline.

4. FEES, PAYMENT & DUES

15. What should I do after online payment? Check that payment and examination registration are reflected in the portal and retain the receipt.

16. What if my account is debited but registration is not reflected? Do not repeatedly make payments without checking the prescribed procedure. Report the transaction details through the notified channel.

17. Can unpaid dues affect results? Yes. Results may be withheld if tuition fees or other Institute dues remain unpaid.

5. HALL TICKET

18. Where can I obtain my Hall Ticket? Download it from the student login or examination portal after release.

19. What should I check? Name, Father Name, Mother Name and Gender Hall Ticket/Roll Number, dates, session, courses, venue and other details.

20. What if there is an error? For Name, Father Name, Mother Name and Gender corrections ,contact the Admission Section immediately.

21. Is the Hall Ticket compulsory? Yes. Carry the Hall Ticket and Student ID Card to every examination.

22. Can I enter without ID? No. Both Hall Ticket and Student ID Card are required.

6. TIMETABLE & EXAMINATION VENUE

23. Where should I check the timetable? Follow the timetable officially notified by the Institute/Examination & Evaluation Section.

24. How early should I reach? At least 15 minutes before commencement.

25. What should I check before entering?

Date, session, venue, seating arrangement, Hall Ticket and ID; ensure no prohibited devices/materials are carried.

7. THEORY EXAMINATION

26. What are duration and marks?

3 hours and 60 marks.

27. What is the pattern? Part-A: 5 compulsory short-answer questions × 2 = 10 marks. Part-B: 10 descriptive questions, two from each unit, with internal choice; answer one from each unit = 50 marks.

28. Does SEE cover the entire syllabus? Yes. All five units carry equal weightage.

29. How should I manage answers? Read carefully, follow the choice pattern, number answers correctly and manage time.

8. EXAMINATION HALL RULES

30. What should I carry? Hall Ticket, Student ID Card and permitted stationery.

31. Which electronic devices are prohibited? Mobile phones, programmable calculators, tablets, laptops, earphones, Bluetooth devices, electronic gadgets and smart/data-storage watches with input/storage capability.

32. Can I keep a switched-off mobile phone inside? Students should not carry prohibited electronic devices into the Examination Hall.

33. Can I talk to another student? No. Maintain silence and do not communicate with other candidates.

34. Can I exchange stationery? No, unless specifically permitted by examination instructions.

35. Can I leave early? Only as permitted by the examination instructions.

9. MALPRACTICE / UNFAIR MEANS

36. What is malpractice? Prohibited conduct during examinations and evaluation activities covered by the applicable Regulations and Malpractice Rules.

37. Does it apply only to SEE? No. It applies to internal assessments, continuous evaluation, SEE, practicals, project viva- voce and online/offline examinations.

38. Are AI/digital technologies covered? Yes. Misuse of AI tools, remote screen sharing, digital platforms, social media, messaging apps or online communication to share, reproduce or access examination material is covered.

39. What happens when a case is reported? It is documented and, where required, referred to the Malpractice Committee. The student is given an opportunity to present an explanation.

40. Who is on the Malpractice Committee? Principal – Chairman; Controller of Examinations; concerned Course HoD; concerned Course Faculty; concerned Student Discipline HoD – Members.

41. Where are exact punishments specified? In the applicable Malpractice Rules/Table A1 of the approved R25 Regulations.

10. RESULTS & GRADE MEMORANDUM

42. Where are results displayed? Through the Institute Examination Portal as prescribed.

43. What is displayed? Marks in each course with CIE and SEE break-up.

44. What is a Memorandum of Grades? It contains course code, course title, registered credits, letter grade and credits earned.

45. Are CIE/SEE marks printed in it? No.

11. GRADES, SGPA & CGPA

46. What is the minimum passing grade? C (40% to less than 50%).

47. What is SGPA? Total earned weighted grade points in a semester divided by total credits in the semester.

48. When is SGPA calculated? Only when all courses registered in that semester are cleared; rounded to two decimal places.

49. From which semester is CGPA calculated? From II Semester onwards.

50. How is final CGPA calculated? At VIII Semester after applying the best-160-credit provision.

51. Are grades below C considered for CGPA? No.

12. SUPPLEMENTARY EXAMINATIONS

52. Who can appear? Students having applicable backlog courses, subject to notified rules and schedule.

53. When are they conducted? Along with the applicable regular SEE.

54. Which courses are covered? During Even Semester SEE: applicable Odd Semester courses; during Odd Semester SEE: applicable Even Semester courses.

55. What happens to internal marks? For F/Ab courses, regular internal marks are retained.

13. DEGREE COMPLETION

56. What are the credit requirements?

164 credits are prescribed; minimum 160 credits must be earned.

57. What is minimum final CGPA?

5.0.

58. Is there a minimum SGPA requirement? Yes, not less than 5.0 in each semester.

59. Do mandatory courses need to be completed? Yes, subject to applicable credit provisions.

60. What is checked before Provisional Certificate processing? Required courses passed, 160 credits, mandatory requirements, final CGPA ≥5.0, semester SGPA requirements, applicable supplementary results, clearance of holds/dues and best 160-credit provision.

14. GRACE MARKS

61. Can grace marks be awarded? They may be awarded after completion of entire coursework where the student has failed in not more than three courses, provided the addition enables passing and degree qualification.

62. Is there a limit? Yes. Total grace marks shall not exceed 0.15% of total maximum marks of all eight semesters, excluding Value Added Courses and Skill Development Courses.

63. Are grace marks automatic? No. They are subject to Institute guidelines and applicable eligibility conditions.
